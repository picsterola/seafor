§ 00 · State of the city

This isn't a slump.
It's a structural problem.

Seattle is objectively near the bottom of major U.S. cities on the metrics that matter to the people who live and work here. State and local policy choices made it this way, and those choices keep compounding. § 01 · Why this exists

One side built infrastructure.
The other side keeps bringing slide decks to gunfights.

Seattle's progressive organizations have built formidable political infrastructure: coordinated candidate pipelines, disproportionately influential media, advocacy machines that consistently shape who gets elected and what gets done.

Pragmatic, middle-of-the-road voices, the broad center of Seattle's electorate, have nothing comparable. Civic groups and business associations work in silos. Candidates who support evidence-based policy run without sustained institutional backing.

The other side doesn't just win elections. It has built the permanent infrastructure that makes winning routine, at the ballot box and at the dais. Until the pragmatic center builds something equivalent, the cycle keeps repeating: a moderate gets elected on a wave, governs alone, and gets picked off in the next primary. The infrastructure deficit is the problem.

Not

another campaign committee that disappears after November.

Not

a rebrand of an existing org with a fresh logo and the same instincts.

Not

a vehicle for any one candidate, faction, or business interest.

Is

permanent civic infrastructure for the pragmatic center. Built to outlast every cycle.

SeaFor is the infrastructure play. Not a campaign vehicle with better branding. Not a one-cycle committee that disappears after November. A durable, moderate, pro-governance operating system designed to outlast any single election.

§ 02 · The work

Three workstreams. Built to actually ship.

Three things, done well. Here's what we do differently in each, and what "better" actually means in practice. No mission statements. Just the work.

  1. 01

    A weekly call to action, not just a tracker

    The fight happens in committee. Three votes can change a bill before it ever hits the floor. We track the amendments where the real legislating happens, and we hand readers the receipts and the buttons to do something with them.

    Status quo

    Coverage focuses on Full Council passage. By then the bill has been quietly rewritten in a committee nobody outside City Hall watches. Voters learn what changed weeks later, if ever, and the only people who know how to act on it are paid lobbyists and ideologues with a Slack channel.

    SeaFor

    Committee-first coverage with one-click action. Every amendment: who offered it, who voted, what it does. Take Wilson's shelter bill: 24/7 security, buffer zones around schools and daycare, sober shelter requirements. We name it. Then we give you a pre-drafted email to your Councilmember, a shareable card, and an op-ed pitch ready to send.

    • Weekly committee digest. AI-assisted, human-edited. What got introduced, amended, and moved out of committee, in plain English.
    • Amendment receipts. The text, the sponsor, the vote count, and what changed from the original.
    • Take action, in 30 seconds. Email your Councilmember, post the receipts, pitch an op-ed. Pre-drafted, fully editable, sourced.
    • The Brief. A 5-minute weekly readout on what moved at City Hall and what's coming next week.
  2. 02

    Policy oversight

    Real analysis on public safety, homelessness, housing, permitting, and land use. Plus public air cover for the good-governance moves sitting electeds are already trying to make.

    Status quo

    Think tanks publish a 60-page PDF six months after the decision. Advocacy groups put out a one-pager that's 90% adjective. Nobody reads either. Council staff write the actual policy alone, with whatever evidence base they had time to gather on a Tuesday.

    SeaFor

    Issue briefs that arrive before the committee meeting, not after. Real numbers, real comparisons, real tradeoffs. Written so a Councilmember can actually use them and a voter can actually understand them.

    • Permitting & housing. Time-to-permit benchmarks vs. peer cities, line-item delays, what to fix first.
    • Public safety. Staffing math, response times, alternate-response programs, what's working and what isn't.
    • Homelessness response. Per-bed costs, throughput data, KCRHA accountability instead of vibes.
    • Budget readouts that follow the dollar from press release to ordinance to actual outcome.
  3. 03

    Candidate recruitment & electoral support

    Identify, recruit, and develop pragmatic candidates. Sustained backing through affiliated PAC and IE structures with a clean firewall and adult legal review.

    Status quo

    A consultant calls a friend in March of an election year. The candidate is a small business owner with no comms shop, no opposition research, and a website built in a weekend. By July they're on defense. By November they've lost.

    SeaFor

    A multi-cycle bench-building program. We meet candidates before they need us. Training, vetting, narrative work, and credible institutional support that doesn't disappear when the cycle gets ugly.

    • Bench pipeline. Vetted prospects across districts, refreshed each cycle.
    • Candidate bootcamp. Comms, fundraising, voter file, opposition research, debate prep.
    • Affiliated PAC + IE with proper firewalls, public reporting, and outside legal review.
    • Air cover that doesn't fold. Funded through the cycle, not until the first bad poll.
§ 03 · How we're different

A small newsroom
with modern AI tools.

SeaFor runs like a lean newsroom: pitch, draft, report, edit, fact-check, publish. AI does the heavy reading: every committee agenda, every amendment, every fiscal note. Humans run the desk: editors assign, reporters call sources, fact-checkers verify, and nothing ships without a sign-off. Days instead of weeks. Receipts instead of vibes.

  • 01

    An assignment desk that never sleeps

    Every Council and committee agenda gets parsed and tagged the moment it posts. The desk decides what's worth a story, what's worth a graphic, and what's worth a follow-up call. No story falls through the cracks because someone took Friday off.

  • 02

    Reporters, not stenographers

    A draft is a starting point. Reporters work the phones, pull the SEEC filings, and ask the questions the press release tries to dodge. The story changes between draft and publish, the way it should.

  • 03

    Fact-check or kill

    Every claim, number, and quote is verified against primary sources before publish. If we can't source it, we cut it. If we got it wrong, we correct it on the front of the next dispatch. No hallucinations, no slop, no apologies for things we should have caught.

  • 04

    Lean by design

    A small editorial team doing the work of a much larger one. Donor dollars buy reach and rigor, not headcount and rent. The AI is the printing press, not the editor.

§ 06 · Media

Pragmatic center
with a pulse.

There is no reliable Seattle media representing the pragmatic center. The Stranger is what it is. The Times has its moments. Everything in between is either a press-release laundromat or a podcast nobody listens to. The center has been polite for so long it forgot how to be loud. We're done being polite.

Two things, every week. We aggregate the best of the moderate and pragmatic-center work that actually exists, and we publish original commentary that skewers the obviously hard-left media still pretending it speaks for the city. Sarcastic, witty, sourced, and built to generate views. The center can be loud and opinionated. We will not roll over.
